# Paper-track requirements checked 10 October 2026

The official Kaggle API returned the current competition metadata and full
content pages. Raw responses are retained locally in
`runs/official/competition-metadata.json` and `competition-pages.json`.
These are administrative evidence, not benchmark inputs.

- Final deadline: 12 November 2026 at 23:59 UTC, equivalent to Friday
  13 November at 07:59 Australia/Perth. Internal deadline: 11 November.
- Submit an original, unpublished research Writeup of at most 3,000 words.
  Our conservative target includes references and captions within that limit.
- Include title, subtitle, abstract, introduction, methods, experiments,
  related work, and citations.
- The five equally weighted criteria are novelty, quality/generalization,
  relevance, verifiability, and clarity, each scored from zero to five.
- Prize categories are Overall Best Paper ($15,000), Best New Resource
  ($10,000), and Best New Application ($10,000). GraphTrust-SWE targets the
  resource category with an accompanying evidence-based paper.
- Participation in the separate coding-agent competition is optional.
- Public code/notebook/project links are optional for eligibility but are part
  of our reproducibility deliverable. Links must be accessible without login
  or a paywall. Attaching private Kaggle resources to a public Writeup can make
  them public after the deadline; inspect attachments before final submission.
  When providing code, include the public executable notebook in Project Links
  and verify its access from a signed-out session.
- Saving a Writeup does not submit it. Use its Submit action and verify the
  resulting submitted status. Unsubmitted drafts are ineligible.
- Join the competition with the submitting account and verify its joined status
  before submission. Joining, saving a draft and submitting the Writeup are
  separate requirements; none is inferred from running a Kaggle notebook.
- Original code is Apache-2.0; preserve third-party licenses. Regenerate source
  snapshots from public upstreams instead of bundling competition data or
  reference answers into the inference resource.
